Real cost ranges, ongoing costs, hidden costs, and what a $1,000 local build includes.

How much does a small business website cost in 2026? The honest answer is that it depends on who builds it, what it needs to do, and what you pay after launch. Larger or custom builds get a fixed quote after a strategy call.

The four ways to build a small business website

1. DIY website builder

Website builders like Wix, Squarespace, and similar platforms let you build a website yourself from a template. The upfront cost is low, and some offer a free website plan with limits. Monthly plans add up, and paid apps, premium templates, and ecommerce features increase the recurring cost. The biggest cost is your time: design, copywriting, photos, SEO, and updates are all on you.

2. Freelancer

A freelance web designer can build a custom site for less than many agencies. Quality and pricing vary widely. Ask what is included, who hosts the site, and what happens if you need changes after launch.

3. Local web designer or small studio

A local studio usually combines design, copy guidance, and SEO setup, and stays available after launch. This is where Nick Perry Digital fits: a $1,000 standard local build with SEO-ready structure, built for service businesses.

4. Full-service agency

Larger agencies offer strategy, branding, custom design, and development teams. They often charge much more, and are a fit for complex projects with big budgets.

What drives website design costs up

  • Number of pages: more service and location pages take more design and writing time
  • Custom design: unique layouts cost more than adapting a template
  • Ecommerce: product catalogs, payments, and shipping add complexity
  • Integrations: booking systems, CRMs, and payment tools take setup time
  • Copywriting: writing every page from scratch is real work
  • Photography: real photos beat stock but cost time or money
  • Migration: moving content off an old site takes effort
  • Custom features: calculators, portals, and member areas are custom development

Ongoing costs after launch

Many small business owners budget for the build and forget the recurring costs. A realistic budget includes:

  • Domain name: renewed yearly
  • Website hosting: monthly or yearly
  • Security and updates: especially for WordPress sites with plugins
  • Edits: new services, hours, photos, and pages
  • Paid plugins or apps: common on builders and WordPress
  • SEO and marketing: optional, but how most sites get traffic

Our care plan covers hosting, security, updates, and routine edits for $150 per month. Ask about free hosting on eligible static builds.

Hidden costs to watch for

  • A low build price that does not include hosting or edits
  • Templates you cannot leave without rebuilding
  • Sites where the designer owns the domain or files
  • Paid plugins that renew yearly
  • A cheap site that gets no traffic because it was not built for search
  • A full rebuild in two years because the site was never maintained

Is a cheap website worth it?

A cheap website is worth it if it does its job. For a local service business, that job is to show up in local search, make a good impression, and get the phone to ring. A website that looks fine but never gets found is not cheap, because it does not pay for itself.

DIY website vs professional build

A DIY website can work for a hobby or a very early business. For a business that depends on local calls, a professional build usually pays off faster: the structure follows your services, the copy speaks to local customers, and SEO is built in from day one. Your time also has a cost.

How to budget for a small business website

  1. List the pages you need: home, each core service, each main town, about, contact.
  2. Decide whether you need ecommerce or booking.
  3. Ask each quote what is included after launch.
  4. Confirm you will own the domain, site, and content.
  5. Plan for ongoing costs such as hosting, maintenance, and edits.
  6. Decide whether you want ongoing SEO.

Questions to ask before you hire a web designer

  • What exactly does the price include?
  • Who owns the website and domain?
  • Who hosts it, and what does that cost?
  • How are edits handled after launch?
  • Is the site built for local SEO?
  • How long will it take?
