Small business reputation management FAQ

Can you remove bad reviews?

We can report reviews that violate platform policies, but no one can promise removal of genuine reviews.

Which review platforms matter most?

Google first for most local businesses, then Yelp, Facebook, and industry sites.

Why online reputation matters for small businesses

Most customers read online reviews before choosing a local business. A few recent negative reviews with no response, or a rating below competitors, can send customers elsewhere before they ever call. Small business reputation management is the ongoing work of earning positive reviews, responding to every customer review, keeping business listings accurate, and handling problems before they turn into a reputation crisis.

Where your reputation lives

  • Google Business Profile: usually the most visible review site for local businesses
  • Yelp: important for restaurants, shops, and many service businesses
  • Facebook: recommendations and comments
  • Industry review platforms: such as sites for contractors, health care, or legal services
  • Search results: what appears when someone searches your business name
  • AI tools: AI search engines summarize reviews and sentiment when they describe a business

Step 1: monitor reviews

You cannot respond to reviews you do not see. We monitor reviews across the platforms that matter for your business, so new reviews get a timely response.

Step 2: ask for reviews consistently

Most happy customers will not leave a review unless asked. A simple review request system, a text or email with a direct link sent right after the job, steadily builds positive reviews. Consistency matters more than bursts. Never offer payment or discounts for reviews, and never filter requests to only happy customers in ways that break platform policies.

Step 3: respond to reviews

Responding shows customers you care. Thank positive reviewers by name and mention the service if appropriate. For negative reviews, stay calm, acknowledge the concern, avoid sharing private details, and offer to resolve it offline. A thoughtful response to a negative review can build more trust than a perfect rating.

Step 4: deal with fake reviews and spam

Fake reviews from people who were never customers, or reviews that break platform rules, can be reported through each platform's official process. We document and report them. Platforms decide what to remove, so no one can promise removal.

Step 5: keep business listings accurate

Listing management protects your brand reputation. Wrong hours, an old phone number, or a duplicate listing frustrates customers and can lead to bad reviews that were not your fault. We keep business information consistent across directories.

Step 6: learn from feedback

Reviews are free customer research. Patterns in reviews, positive or negative, show what customers value and what to fix. Sentiment analysis, even done by reading reviews monthly, helps business owners improve service and marketing.

Reputation crisis management

Sometimes a wave of negative reviews arrives after a real mistake, a viral post, or a review bombing. Have a plan: one person responds, responses are calm and consistent, the problem is fixed where possible, and fake reviews are reported. Proactive reputation management, with a strong base of positive reviews, makes a crisis easier to survive.

Reputation management software vs a reputation management service

Reputation management tools can automate review requests and monitoring. They are useful, but someone still has to write good responses, fix listings, report spam, and make decisions. A reputation management service handles that work for you. Ours is included in our monthly SEO plans.

How reviews affect local SEO

Google has said review count and rating can factor into local ranking, as part of prominence. More recent, positive, detailed reviews help your Google Business Profile compete in the map pack. That is why we treat reputation management and local SEO as one plan.

Proactive reputation management tips for business owners

  1. Ask every satisfied customer for a review the same day.
  2. Make the request easy with a direct link.
  3. Respond to every review within a few days.
  4. Never argue in public.
  5. Fix the root cause of repeated complaints.
  6. Keep listings accurate on every platform.
  7. Report fake reviews through official channels.
  8. Check your business name in search results monthly.
