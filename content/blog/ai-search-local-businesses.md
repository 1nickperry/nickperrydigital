Clients have started asking whether ChatGPT or Google's AI answers will recommend them. It is a fair question. It is also a question with a less magical answer than the ads for "AI SEO" suggest.

## They repeat a public record

An assistant does not have a secret folder of the best roofer in Delta. It leans on pages, listings, reviews, and directories it can reach. If those sources disagree about your name or your phone number, the model has nothing solid to say. Sometimes it says nothing. Sometimes it mixes you up with a similar company.

That is why AI search work starts in the same place as local SEO: one real listing, a site that describes the services in plain language, and directories that are not stuck in 2017.

## They like a direct answer

A page that opens with your company history and never states the towns you serve is hard to quote. A page that says what you do, where you do it, and how to hire you is easier. FAQs help when they are real questions. They do not help when they are stuffed with a city name and no information.

The town pages on this site are written that way on purpose. Each one says what the work costs and answers a few local questions. They are not a stack of doorway pages.

## Monitoring is limited

I can check whether a tool mentions you for a query I choose to run. I cannot watch every conversation a customer has with an assistant, and I cannot force the next answer to include you. The $1,500 plan includes an AI visibility check, ongoing optimization, and local outreach. It does not include a guaranteed mention. I would rather lose the sale than pretend otherwise.

## What to do if you are curious

Read your own homepage as if you had never heard of the company. Could a stranger tell the service, the town, and the next step? Then read the Google listing. If those two do not match, fix that before you buy a specialized AI retainer.
