A Western Slope customer often meets you on Google Maps, not on your homepage. If the hours are wrong or the primary category is a guess, an ad just sends more people to a confused listing.

This is the order I use. It is not a secret checklist. It is the boring work that makes the rest of the marketing possible.

## Confirm you are the owner

The profile should live in an account your business controls. Former agencies, old employees, and duplicate managers need to be removed or reduced to the right role. If the profile is suspended, fix that before you decorate it. A pretty description does not unsuspend a listing.

## Use one listing for one real location

Service-area businesses can set the towns they drive to. They should not open a second profile for every town on the highway. A Grand Junction company that also works in Fruita still has one front door. Say so on the website. Do not invent a Fruita storefront.

## Make the record match the website

Name, phone, and address should match the site and the directories that already mention you. Categories should describe the work you want, not every side job you have ever done. Services on the listing should be services you will actually accept a call for.

## Photos and posts come after the facts

Add real photos of the work, the truck, or the shop. Skip stock scenes that could belong to any city. Weekly posts are useful once the categories and services are right. They are not a substitute for a wrong phone number.

## Ask for reviews without scripting them

After a job is finished, send one short link. Do not tell people which star to tap, and do not ask friends to pretend they hired you. Reply to the reviews you get. Patterns in the comments are also free research for the website.

## Then look at ads

If you still want Google Ads, you will at least be sending people to a listing and a site that agree. Ads management is a separate quote from the monthly SEO plans. Local Services Ads, where your category qualifies, are a different product and sit inside those plans.
