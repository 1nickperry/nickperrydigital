The website build I sell includes 90 days of SEO after launch. That window is easy to misunderstand. It is not a promise that you will own the map pack on day 12. It is the period when the new site and the Google listing get a fair chance to be understood.

## Week one is housekeeping

The site goes live on a domain you own. The important pages have titles, the phone number works on a phone, and the form lands somewhere you actually read. The Google Business Profile points at the new site. Old URLs redirect if you replaced an existing site. Search Console gets the sitemap.

None of that feels like marketing. Skipping it is how a new site sits invisible while everyone stares at the design.

## The middle is alignment

Directories, the listing, and the homepage should tell the same story about your name and your services. I would rather fix three conflicts than publish ten thin blog posts. If a town page exists, it should say something true about that town. Swapping a city name into the same paragraph is not the work.

Reviews, if you are collecting them, start in this stretch. They will not transform a listing overnight. They do compound.

## The end of the 90 days is a decision

You will have a site, a listing that matches it, and a record of what was changed. You can stop. You can add the $150 care plan for hosting and small edits. Or you can move to a monthly plan at $850 or $1,500 if you want the listing and the site tended every month.

Nothing in that choice is automatic. There is no long-term contract hiding at the end of the build.

## What I will not do in those 90 days

I will not invent traffic charts to make the window look successful. If the honest report is "Google has indexed the site and the listing is clean, and calls have not moved yet," that is the report. Some categories and some towns move faster than others. Pretending otherwise is how this industry got a bad reputation.
